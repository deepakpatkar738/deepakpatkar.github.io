Dr. Deepak Patkar

Post-doctoral Fellow · Theoretical & Computational Chemistry

I am a theoretical and computational chemist with research interests spanning quantum chemistry, molecular modeling, and interdisciplinary applications across chemistry, physics, and biology. My PhD research focused on non-covalent interactions, hydrogen-bond cooperativity, and molecular cluster energetics, applying the Molecular Tailoring Approach (MTA) to investigate subtle interaction energies and many-body cooperative effects in complex molecular systems. Currently a Postdoctoral Fellow at the Fujian Institute of Research on the Structure of Matter, I work on copper–peptide mediated liquid–liquid phase separation and biomolecular interactions involving transition metals, combining electronic structure methods and computational modeling to understand chemical and biological phenomena at the molecular level. My broader expertise spans transition metal and main-group chemistry, catalysis, molecular simulations, and machine learning in chemoinformatics, bioinformatics, and materials informatics bridging quantum chemical methods with data-driven approaches to address problems across interdisciplinary fields.

The Story of Hydrogen Bonds
HISTORY OF CHEMISTRY · MOLECULAR INTERACTIONS · 2024
In 1912, T.S. Moore and T.F. Winmill introduced the concept of the hydrogen bond in their study "The State of Amines in Aqueous Solution," marking the beginning of a century-long exploration into this fundamental chemical interaction. Hydrogen bonds, primarily involving nitrogen, oxygen, or fluorine atoms, are pivotal in various scientific fields. They significantly influence the properties of water, stabilize the structures of DNA and proteins, and play a crucial role in supramolecular chemistry.

Over the past hundred years, our understanding of hydrogen bonds has deepened, revealing their complexity and versatility. Initially recognized in simple systems, hydrogen bonds are now known to be integral to complex biological processes and advanced material designs. As we reflect on a century of hydrogen bond research, we acknowledge its profound impact on science and anticipate future discoveries that will further elucidate its role in the natural world.
Moore, T.S.; Winmill, T.F. J. Chem. Soc. Trans. 1912, 101, 1635–1676 · Jeffrey, G.A. An Introduction to Hydrogen Bonding, Oxford University Press, 1997
